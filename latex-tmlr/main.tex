\begin{abstract}
Quantizing a proposer's weights can leave every metric a discovery loop watches unchanged while collapsing the variation the loop depends on. On a constructive geometry task at 14B, degrading served quantization moves viability and validity by no detectable amount (non-rejection at $n = 50$ per rung, not demonstrated equivalence; at 7B viability instead \emph{inverts}), while at the 2-bit rung the proposer largely stops departing from its parent. Two registered outcomes carry the claim. An echo bound released before the run that tested it --- \texttt{q2\_k >= 60\%}, \texttt{q4\_k\_m <= 35\%} --- held on five never-sampled seeds, at \textbf{79\% (19/24)} coordinate-verified parent-echo against \textbf{6\% (1/17)}; and a must-differ probe returned coordinate-identical copies in \textbf{5 of 5} valid outputs under an explicit instruction not to copy. A wider-looking contrast --- \textbf{94\% (17/18)} against \textbf{14\% (8/57)} across the upper three rungs --- comes from a re-execution rather than the registered wave and is reported second for that reason (\S3). The failed proposals are coherent, well-formed near-copies, which is why every pass/fail instrument reports health. A post-hoc loop-level accounting shows what that costs: the 2-bit rung takes 1 accepted hill-climb step in 50 calls against 14--16 at the upper rungs, while final best score separates the rungs nowhere --- the outcome number a practitioner reports is blind to an order-of-magnitude collapse in search activity. A fixed-parent control bounds the result rather than extending it (direction and the 2-bit endpoint survive; the spread does not), a scheme-versus-bit-width control returns its inconclusive branch, and six further registered waves bound generality: a capability-tier control returns DISSOCIATION, a second geometry preserves the rung contrast but refutes the absolute bands, and two tasks plus five non-Qwen families sit below their own registered competence floors. A sixth family, screen-selected at 31B, is the first non-Qwen arm in the programme to clear the upper rung's control floor (4.8 accepted steps per seed, every seed improving) and reproduces the 2-bit search-activity collapse under a registered floor-gated \emph{secondary} --- 0.0 accepted steps against 4.8, with viability intact at both rungs --- while its echo primary is underpowered (zero valid 2-bit outputs against a 20-valid floor) and the collapse channel differs: well-formed invalidity rather than parent echo, so the copying signature, though no longer the search collapse, remains unresolved beyond Qwen. Everything beyond the two registered outcomes is descriptive or post-hoc and labelled as such in \S3. The second half asks what this means for studies that address models through managed agent runtimes by \emph{alias}. Served quantization is one of several serving-path variables an alias leaves unattested, and it is now one the dependent measure is known to be sensitive to; our ladder varies only the SHA-256-pinned weight file, so it measures served-weight precision, not a decode-path effect. A forensic case study of an arm addressed only as \texttt{opus\_alias} --- serving and behavioral signatures that \emph{mildly favour} an unattested serving path without deciding it --- becomes untestable within six days: re-issuing byte-identical prompts returns \textbf{30/30 valid against the original 4/30}, disconfirming the registered stability bound at the widest margin the design allows, and the alias no longer addresses the call under study. We close with a repair protocol audited against itself --- prompt hashing before sampling, verbatim raw storage, dated alias maps, hash-locked preregistration --- including one proposed instrument we tested against our own rows and withdrew.
\end{abstract}

\subsubsection*{Broader Impact Statement}

This paper reports a way for a quantized proposer to keep every health metric a
discovery loop watches while the search underneath it collapses. Two readings
would be misuses of that result. It is not an argument against quantization:
quantized serving is what puts capable models within reach of researchers
without datacentre budgets, and nothing here shows harm at the rungs most
practitioners use --- the collapse we measure is at the 2-bit endpoint, on one
task family, at one scale. Nor is it an argument that published loop results are
wrong; they are unrepeatable in a specific and now-demonstrable way, which
calls for disclosure rather than retraction. The costs of the repair protocol in
\S6 fall hardest on exactly the researchers least able to absorb them, and we
have tried to keep every item in it cheap and local.

Two further repercussions are worth naming. The forensic method of \S4 --- reading
a serving path from latency and behaviour --- is a capability as well as a
diagnostic, and the same reasoning that lets a researcher ask what served their
calls could let a third party probe an endpoint its operator would rather not
have characterized; we think the transparency gain dominates here, because the
alternative is a research literature that cannot audit its own substrate, but
the direction is not costless. Finally, this work addresses a commercial alias
whose behaviour changed mid-study. We report that as an observability failure of
the addressing mode, common to managed runtimes generally, and not as a claim
about any vendor's conduct; we have no evidence of intent and make no
allegation of any.
