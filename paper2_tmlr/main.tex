\begin{abstract}
Quantizing a proposer's weights can leave every metric a discovery loop
watches unchanged while collapsing the variation the loop depends on. On a
constructive geometry task at 14B, degrading served quantization moves
viability and validity by no detectable amount (non-rejection at $n=50$ per
rung, not demonstrated equivalence; at 7B viability instead \emph{inverts}),
while at the 2-bit rung the proposer largely stops departing from its parent.
Two registered outcomes carry the claim. An echo bound released before the
run that tested it --- \texttt{q2\_k} $\geq 60\%$, \texttt{q4\_k\_m} $\leq
35\%$ --- held on five never-sampled seeds, at \textbf{79\% (19/24)}
coordinate-verified parent-echo against \textbf{6\% (1/17)}; and a
must-differ probe returned coordinate-identical copies in \textbf{5 of 5}
valid outputs under an explicit instruction not to copy. A wider-looking
contrast --- \textbf{94\% (17/18)} against \textbf{14\% (8/57)} across the
upper three rungs --- comes from a re-execution rather than the registered
wave and is reported second for that reason (\S3). The failed proposals are
coherent, well-formed near-copies, which is why every pass/fail instrument
reports health. A post-hoc loop-level accounting shows what that costs: the
2-bit rung takes 1 accepted hill-climb step in 50 calls against 14--16 at
the upper rungs, while final best score separates the rungs nowhere --- the
outcome number a practitioner reports is blind to an order-of-magnitude
collapse in search activity. A fixed-parent control bounds the result rather
than extending it (direction and the 2-bit endpoint survive; the spread does
not), a scheme-versus-bit-width control returns its inconclusive branch, and
five further registered waves bound generality: a capability-tier control
returns DISSOCIATION, a second geometry preserves the rung contrast but
refutes the absolute bands, and two tasks plus four non-Qwen families sit
below their own registered competence floors, leaving family generality
unresolved. Everything beyond the two registered outcomes is descriptive or
post-hoc and labelled as such in \S3.

The second half asks what this means for studies that address models through
managed agent runtimes by \emph{alias}. Served quantization is one of
several serving-path variables an alias leaves unattested, and it is now one
the dependent measure is known to be sensitive to; our ladder varies only
the SHA-256-pinned weight file, so it measures served-weight precision, not
a decode-path effect. A forensic case study of an arm addressed only as
\texttt{opus\_alias} --- serving and behavioral signatures that \emph{mildly
favour} an unattested serving path without deciding it --- becomes
untestable within six days: re-issuing byte-identical prompts returns
\textbf{30/30 valid against the original 4/30}, disconfirming the registered
stability bound at the widest margin the design allows, and the alias no
longer addresses the call under study. We close with a repair protocol
audited against itself --- prompt hashing before sampling, verbatim raw
storage, dated alias maps, hash-locked preregistration --- including one
proposed instrument we tested against our own rows and withdrew.
\end{abstract}
